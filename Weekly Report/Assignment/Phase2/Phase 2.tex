\subsection{Gathering Data}
\label{subsec:Gathering Data}
\noindent
The dataset was obtained from \href{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/austinreese/usa-housing-listings}{Kaggle}. According to the dataset author, the data was scraped from Craigslist in approximately January 2020 and compiled into a single CSV file. The raw file has the following dimensions:
\vspace{-12pt}
\begin{itemize} [itemsep = -12pt]
    \item File size: 558.44 MB
    \item Total rows: 384,977
    \item Total columns: 22
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Describing Data}
\noindent
The dataset contains a mix of numeric, categorical, text, and geographic feature types. The target variable for this project is price.

\subsection{Exploring Data}
\noindent
Initial exploratory analysis revealed the following key patterns and distributions within the dataset. Apartments are the most common property type, followed by houses and townhouses. Most properties fall below the third quartile values of 1,150 square feet and \$1,395 in price, and most listings are below two bathrooms and two bedrooms at the third quartile level. The dataset is described as largely pet friendly, while furnished listings, EV charging availability, and wheelchair accessibility are all reported as minority cases. About one quarter of listings are noted as smoke friendly.

\subsubsection{Anomalies and Outliers}
\vspace{-12pt}
\begin{itemize} [itemsep = -9pt]
    \item Properties listed at \$0 were identified and removed. These may represent listings open to negotiation, but they introduce noise into price modeling.
    \item Properties with less than 1 sq ft were identified and removed.
    \item Properties with 0 bedrooms and 0 bathrooms were retained, as these may represent valid land rental listings.
    \item Price outliers were noted and will be addressed in Section 3.2 %addressed in Section \ref{subsec:Cleaning Data}.
\end{itemize}
